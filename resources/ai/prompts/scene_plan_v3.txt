BUILD STATE IS NOT AN IDENTITY INVARIANT

The design does not change. The shape, proportion, layout and permanent openings this object is being built to are fixed, and any part you describe must match that design.

What a camera sees does change. Early on the outline in frame is a bare bottom structure; later a plated hull; later still a hull carrying a superstructure. The silhouette of one frame is not the silhouette of the finished object, and advancing it is exactly what your delta is for.

So: a part that exists keeps the geometry the design gives it, and which parts exist is yours to advance, one authorised change at a time.

In a continuation edit every structure already built stays present and stays where it is, unless your delta names the alteration.

MILESTONE COVERAGE

You receive a profile listing milestone groups in order, and each group lists its milestones in order. Every milestone you name must come from that profile, spelled exactly. You may not invent one.

Every milestone marked required must be covered by at least one scene somewhere in the plan. A scene may cover more than one milestone when they are visible in the same physical state at the same moment, up to the limit the profile gives. Do not group work that happens at different times just to return fewer scenes.

`phase` is the key of the group a milestone belongs to. All milestones in one scene must come from that same group.

Use the milestones in the order the profile gives them. A later scene may repeat the last milestone of the scene before it to show more of the same step, but it may never step back to an earlier one.

HOW MANY SCENES

The profile states a minimum and the application states a maximum. Inside that range the count is yours, and it follows from how far the work has to be broken down, not from a round number.

A milestone that happens over time usually needs more than one scene: frames go up a few at a time, plating closes a hull section by section. A milestone that happens once needs one scene: a vessel is launched once.

If the planning requirements carry a target duration and a clip length, the number of scenes should come out near the target divided by the clip length. Treat that as direction, not as a rule: covering every required milestone matters more than hitting the number. If the requirements carry no duration, ignore this paragraph entirely.

Do not pad the plan to reach a number and do not compress two real events into one scene to keep it short.

CONTINUITY GROUPS

Decide how the scenes share a viewpoint before you write any prompt.

A continuity group is a run of consecutive scenes shot from the same viewpoint in the
same setting. The first scene of a group is a hard cut and establishes that viewpoint;
every scene after it in the group is a continuation and is generated from the keyframe
of the scene immediately before it, so it inherits the structure already built.

Prefer to keep a viewpoint while the next piece of work is still clearly visible from
it. Change the viewpoint when the current one would hide the operation, when the work
moves to another compartment, or when the setting itself changes. A time jump does not
by itself require a new group: the same viewpoint can show later progress.

A new viewpoint may also reveal useful detail, confirm progress, or serve the pacing,
even when the previous view still shows the work. Explain that benefit in
camera_change_reason.

A group may hold a single scene. Do not split a group only to vary the picture, and do
not keep a viewpoint that hides the work. State the trade-off honestly in
camera_change_reason: opening a group costs the inherited keyframe.

Whatever you decide, work already finished must not disappear at a group boundary. A
hard cut is generated from the approved design anchor, which carries the design but no
build progress, so its delta must describe every accumulated structure the frame shows.

continuity_group        a short lowercase code for the run, like `hull_from_stern`

TRANSITION MODE

Every scene declares one `transition_mode`, and it decides both the preservation block and which image the renderer feeds in.

`continuation_edit` — this scene continues the previous one in the same place, seen from the same camera. The renderer supplies the previous approved keyframe as the leading input. The block holds the camera, framing, lighting, setting and every structure already built. Your delta describes only the authorised visible change from that frame. Do not describe the camera or the setting: they are inherited, and describing them again competes with the block.

`hard_cut_edit` — this scene starts a new situation: a different place, a different time, or the first frame of the story. The renderer supplies the approved identity anchor as the leading input and, when selected, matching reference views as supporting inputs, instead of a previous scene frame. The block holds only the subject's permanent identity and releases camera, setting, lighting and composition to you. Your delta must therefore describe the complete visible state required in this frame, together with its setting, camera and composition — there is no previous frame to inherit from.

The first scene is always `hard_cut_edit`. Use it again when the setting or viewpoint must change to show the work clearly. Prefer `continuation_edit` only when the existing camera can actually see the next operation. Do not keep a distant exterior view for concealed technical or interior work. A hard cut must establish the accumulated build state explicitly; the identity anchor is not a record of previous scene progress. A hard cut may change viewpoint, location or time. Establish a setting consistent with the declared build stage. Explain omitted progress in purpose; do not silently reverse it.

Keep documentary style, natural color balance and credible materials consistent across scenes. Within a continuation, inherit camera, lighting and setting. Across a hard cut, establish the new view and lighting deliberately rather than inventing a different visual style.

THE KEYFRAME AND THE CLIP

Each scene produces one still keyframe, and then one clip that starts from that keyframe.

`delta` describes the keyframe: the complete visible state at one instant, as a camera would see it. Write no motion in it. "The crane lowers the frame onto its supports" is a clip; "the frame hangs above its supports, the lifting chains taut" is a keyframe.

`video` describes what moves onward from that keyframe. It never repeats work the keyframe already shows as finished, and it never asks for a different camera: the clip is shot from exactly where the keyframe was taken.

`video.preserve` specifies which properties remain unchanged. It must not freeze the position, visibility, occupancy or other property that `video.action` explicitly changes. When a panel fills an opening, preserve the surrounding frame geometry, not the opening's empty occupancy.

Your delta is measured against the image the renderer feeds in, never against where the previous clip ended. For a `continuation_edit` that image is the previous scene's approved keyframe — the state its `scene_state` names. If the previous clip finished work this frame depends on, your delta must describe that work too: the renderer never sees the clip.

STATE LABELS

Three short lowercase tokens, letters digits and underscores:

`state_before`  the physical condition entering this scene
`scene_state`   the condition frozen in the keyframe
`end_state`     the condition at the end of the clip

They are your own vocabulary. Keep it consistent across the plan.

For a `continuation_edit`, `state_before` is the previous scene's `scene_state` — the state frozen in its keyframe — not its `video.end_state`. The renderer feeds the approved keyframe, so that is the image the next scene actually continues from.

BASIS

`source_supported` — the source material states that this step happened.
`inferred_process` — the source is silent and you are supplying an ordinary step of how such an object gets built.

`basis` describes the milestone, not every detail of the frame. Use source_supported only when the source supports all milestones claimed by that scene; otherwise use inferred_process. Add only staging needed to make the operation understandable and physically plausible. Do not invent unrelated props, luxury features, brand palettes or extra story beats. Approved design instructions govern the intended design; a product name alone is not evidence of a dimension.

Mark it honestly. A reviewer decides whether to keep an inferred step; a step wrongly marked as sourced removes that decision from them.

THE DELTA

Write it in the present tense as the complete state that must be true in this frame. Two to four short sentences read better than one long one. At most 1000 characters, and shorter when the change is simple.

The delta must be self-contained relative to its declared transition mode and the renderer input defined by that mode. It may not depend on planning context that the application does not store or supply at render time.

For a hard cut, organize the description as setting, visible subject/state, useful material details, viewpoint and lighting. For a continuation, describe the visible changes and their local placement without restating the inherited scene. Use concise labeled lines if they improve clarity; a change-only instruction is acceptable when the requested result is one still state.

Show a photorealistic documentary scene, with real material texture and readable scale. Interiors are visible only through genuine openings or an explicitly established interior viewpoint. No cutaway or transparent hull. Do not add an opening merely to reveal otherwise hidden work.

PROJECT RENDERING RULES

These are local authoring rules, not general provider requirements. The image documentation permits negative constraints and describes image roles. Limited local experiments do not establish that negation or reference labels never work.

1. Prefer the required visible condition over a long prohibition list, such as an empty berth or matte unpainted plating. Include a short exclusion when essential to prevent ambiguity. Neither positive nor negative wording guarantees compliance; outputs still need review.

2. Leave pixel dimensions and aspect ratio to the application. Describe the viewpoint and required visible subject, not an independently chosen canvas that could conflict with render parameters.

3. Do not invent numbered image labels in delta. Actual reference selection, order and role binding belong to the application after planning. Use only the source semantics defined by transition_mode; never assume an unselected supporting image is available.

4. Do not restate numerical dimensions, tier counts or ratios in delta. Preserve the approved design through the supplied identity guidance and references. The generic preservation block does not itself contain those numerical facts.
