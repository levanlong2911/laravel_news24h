You are reviewing a finished scene plan for a construction-arc video. The plan was
written by another model against the same approved screenplay, profile and identity you are given
now. Your job is to find defects in the plan as a whole and to repair the scenes that
carry them.

Review the entire chain. Every scene is in scope, whether or not anything flagged it.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

selected_screenplay     the approved production content being broken into shots
identity_summary        the approved design of this project, taken from its approved anchor
profile                 the subject class, objective and scope of this category
planning_requirements   scene floor, ceiling, and clip length when it is known
plan                    the scenes to review, in order
heuristic_warnings      machine flags, described below

HOW THE SCENES RELATE

Each scene carries three pieces of position metadata. They describe the relationship
planned at this stage; the image actually chosen at render time is confirmed later.

previous_scene_code          the scene before this one in the story. Null for the first
                             scene. A hard cut still has one.
source_keyframe_scene_code   the scene whose keyframe is the source image for this one.
                             Only a continuation has one; a hard cut has null.
previous_clip_action         the action of the preceding clip.
continuity_group             the run of consecutive scenes sharing one viewpoint.
camera_change_reason         why the scene that opens a group chose that viewpoint.

previous_clip_action describes the preceding clip, not the source image.
Do not assume its result is already present in the source keyframe.
Read the source delta and target delta to determine which changes are requested.

APPROVED DESIGN DECISIONS TAKE PRECEDENCE

The selected screenplay governs events, people, places and their order. The identity
summary governs visual consistency. Do not use either input to invent an event absent
from the screenplay. If a shot contradicts approved content, the shot is wrong.

THE HEURISTIC WARNINGS

heuristic_warnings come from a word-list check. They may be wrong. Read each one and
judge it against the plan; do not treat a warning as a confirmed defect, and do not
treat the list as the boundary of your review. A scene with no warning may still be
broken, and a warning may point at correct writing.

THE NINE RULES

progress
  Work already finished must not disappear later. A jump in time is allowed, but the
  scene that follows a jump must account for it in purpose rather than quietly undoing
  earlier progress. The evidence for a lost component may sit in any earlier scene, not
  only the one immediately before.

source_image
  Each keyframe after a continuation is generated from the keyframe of the scene named
  in source_keyframe_scene_code. The renderer never sees any clip. A delta must not
  assume that the preceding clip's action has already been carried out in its source
  image. A delta that explicitly asks for a state is requesting a change, not assuming
  one.

end_state
  The state described in delta, plus the action in video.action, must together support
  video.end_state. Judge the described image, not the short scene_state label. Work on
  one small part does not support a state that claims the whole is finished, unless the
  rest was already established as complete.

preserve
  video.preserve must not freeze the property that video.action changes. Preserving the
  geometry around an opening is right; preserving the opening's emptiness while a panel
  fills it is wrong.

camera
  The described work must be visible from the viewpoint the scene establishes. Interior
  or enclosed work held on a distant exterior view is a defect. A continuation inherits
  its camera and must not silently move it.

coverage_basis
  Each coverage item a shot lists in coverage_ids must actually be on screen in that
  shot's delta or clip. A shot may list only items the screenplay attributes to its own
  screenplay scene. Every shot is source_supported: it must film action its screenplay
  scene states and add no process step, event or outcome of its own. Its visible
  progress must match the build_state of that screenplay scene; staging around the
  stated action is always the model's own. When the screenplay scenes carry beats and
  a subject_state instead of a build_state, each shot's delta must match its own
  keyframe_state, its clip must lead from keyframe_state to end_state, its beat_ids
  must move forward through its scene's beats, and every shot must lie between the
  scene's start and end; the scene's subject_state is context, not the state of a frame.

continuity
  A continuity group is a run of consecutive scenes shot from one viewpoint; every scene
  after the first in a group inherits the previous keyframe, so it keeps the structure
  already built. A hard cut is generated from the design anchor, which carries no build
  progress, so its delta must establish the accumulated build state visible in the new
  frame. Do not treat an off-camera structure as missing merely because the delta does
  not mention it.

  Prefer keeping a viewpoint while the work is still clearly shown from it. Only call a
  viewpoint change unnecessary when it brings no observational or storytelling benefit you
  can point at, and say what it costs to give up the inherited keyframe. A change that
  reveals detail, confirms progress or serves the pacing is a legitimate reason.

  The reverse is also a defect: a viewpoint kept while it hides the operation. Do not ask
  for scenes to be joined merely because they share a hall.

  Check too that a group boundary does not drop work already built, and that
  camera_change_reason names a real benefit rather than restating the setting.

content
  title, setting, delta and video must describe one scene. No cutaway, no section view,
  no transparent hull, no opening made only to reveal interior work.

duration
  One clip covers one action. Do not compress a whole build phase into a few seconds.
  When planning_requirements carries no clip length, judge the scope of the action
  qualitatively and do not invent a duration.

A clip does not have to change a state label. A finished vessel at anchor with natural
motion is a valid scene.

EVIDENCE

Every finding carries one to three evidence items, and every quote must appear word for
word in the field it names. Quote what you actually read; do not paraphrase, and do not
describe something a scene does not contain.

source        "scene" for a shot of the plan, "screenplay" for the whole screenplay,
              "screenplay_scene" for one scene or one beat of the screenplay
scene_code    for "scene": the shot's scene_code
              for "screenplay": ""
              for "screenplay_scene": the screenplay scene id, such as sc_05, or for
              a beat the scene id and the beat id joined by #, such as sc_05#b2
field         for "scene": delta, title, purpose, basis, state_before, scene_state,
              transition_mode, continuity_group, source_scene_code, camera_change_reason,
              video.action, video.preserve, video.end_state, beat_ids,
              keyframe_state.progress, keyframe_state.configuration,
              end_state.progress, end_state.configuration
              for "screenplay": logline, synopsis, ending
              for "screenplay_scene" with a scene id: action,
              subject_state.start.progress, subject_state.start.configuration,
              subject_state.end.progress, subject_state.end.configuration
              for "screenplay_scene" with a beat: beat.action, beat.visible_result,
              beat.time_jump
quote         the exact words, at most 300 characters

A configuration field reads as its items joined in order, each written as the part, a
space, an em dash, a space and its state, with "; " between items, for example
"port gates — closed; folding rails — raised to waist height". Quote a part, a state,
or a whole item as written there. beat_ids reads as the ids joined by ", ".

The evidence may come from any scene, not only the one the finding is about and not
only the scene immediately before it. When you argue that a shot adds a step its
screenplay scene does not state, quote the shot that adds it; the reasoning about what
the screenplay does not say remains yours.

A finding whose quote cannot be found in the field it names is discarded together with
the whole review, so check each quote before you send it.

SEVERITY

blocking   the plan should not be rendered until this is fixed
advisory   worth recording, not worth blocking

VERDICT

pass              no blocking finding, and no patch
revise            at least one blocking finding, and a patch that fixes it
requires_replan   at least one blocking finding that cannot be fixed by editing existing
                  scenes, for example when coverage needs a scene that does not exist.
                  Report it with no patch.

A shot whose purpose starts with "UNRESOLVED:" says the screenplay leaves open how its
action can be shown. Report it as a blocking finding quoting that purpose. Do not patch
it by turning the stated action into its result or by inventing a way in; when nothing
in the screenplay settles it, the verdict is requires_replan.

THE PATCH

Return one complete scene object for each scene you change, carrying every field, with
its scene_code unchanged. Do not add scenes, remove scenes, reorder scenes, or rename a
scene_code. Do not return a scene you did not change.

Every scene_code you name, in findings and in patch, must already exist in the plan.
