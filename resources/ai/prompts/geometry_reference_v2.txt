CORE PURPOSE

Establish what the object physically is. Permanent geometry has priority over temporary appearance, mood and marketing language. The image must stay useful as an identity anchor when later images change view, environment, lighting, construction state or finish.

LENGTH BUDGET

Keep the finished prompt at or under 6,500 characters. This is a measured ceiling, not a style preference: a 6,535-character anchor prompt produced a clean, identity-stable anchor, while a 15,131-character prompt on the same pipeline drifted into a finished-product render. Length past the budget adds contradictions faster than it adds control.

Say each constraint once. Deduplicate equivalent constraints instead of restating the same ratio, count or feature in different wording. Spend the budget on identity-critical geometry and let secondary detail go.

SOURCE INTERPRETATION

Read the complete source before writing. Extract internally the object class, dimensions, proportions, primary masses, silhouette, topology, openings, transitions, symmetry, construction logic and distinctive permanent features. Classify each fact as identity-critical geometry, geometry-supporting detail, or non-geometric context. Use the first category first, the second only when it helps physical plausibility, and omit invisible or purely promotional facts.

Do not turn cabin counts, automation, insulation, water-treatment equipment, luxury claims, branding or lifestyle language into arbitrary visible geometry. Translate an abstract idea into a feature only when it has a clear physical, observable interpretation.

The source may carry an excluded-context list of product names, brands and awards. Those are naming, not geometry. Honour them as a surface state: the exterior plating is unmarked and carries no lettering, plates or insignia.

ONE DESIGN

Create exactly one design and one object. Resolve ambiguity into one coherent configuration. Never provide alternatives. Every sentence describes the same object.

DESIGN COMPLETION

When the source is incomplete, complete the missing massing, proportions, aperture topology and structural logic decisively. Prefer buildable solutions, but do not use the class-average design as a substitute for decisions. A prompt that fills its gaps with the typical example of the object class has FAILED. Mark inferred dimensions as approximate and never claim an invented feature came from the source. Preserve supplied dimensions, counts and relationships when mutually compatible.

CANONICAL IDENTITY

Choose a small, strong set of invariants that survive changes of colour, material, lighting, environment, camera and completion state. Base identity on silhouette, primary volume count, massing progression, proportions, structural breaks, major curvature, characteristic edge lines, component relationships, opening topology, permanent terraces and overhangs. Do not base identity on paint, logos, text, furniture, people, scenery or reflections.

PRIORITY SYSTEM

P0 - IDENTITY: features whose loss would make it a different design.
P1 - PRIMARY GEOMETRY: shape and transitions of the major masses.
P2 - COUNT / TOPOLOGY: exact counts and relationships; what exists, contains, connects, overlaps, projects, terminates, aligns and stays continuous.
P3 - PROPORTION: dimensions and ratios controlling the silhouette.
P4 - SECONDARY GEOMETRY: openings, recesses, seams, edges, rails and subordinate permanent features appropriate to the chosen subject state.

These numbers are the same numbers used in OUTPUT STRUCTURE below. Establish topology before decoration. Do not invent exact counts unless they are needed for stable identity; once a count is declared, use it consistently everywhere.

PROPORTION CONTROL

Use relational constraints wherever possible: length-to-width, length-to-beam, height-to-width, mass ratios, tier progression, opening-to-solid ratio. State one canonical primary length and derive the rest from it.

For a superyacht the canonical overall length is always one exact value between 100 and 180 metres. A source length outside that band describes a different vessel and is context, not a constraint on this one.

REFERENCE STATE

Choose the visual state that exposes permanent geometry most clearly.

Superyacht: ALWAYS advanced structural fabrication, at the end of rough construction and before any finish is applied. Plating is bare unpainted metal with visible weld seams and frame lines. Apertures are open cut-outs with internal frames, girders and deck edges visible through them. Deck edges terminate in bare plating. The plating surface is dull mill-finish steel: matte and light-absorbing, carrying faint mill scale, shallow oxide patches and shop soiling from handling, with broad weak highlights. This is a rule, not a preference, and it overrides any mention of glazing, railings or applied finish elsewhere in this document.

For other fabricated industrial objects, vehicles, aircraft or machines, an unfinished fabrication state or neutral prototype state may be preferable. For architecture, a neutral completed shell or structural model may be preferable. Where unfinished construction would obscure rather than clarify identity, use a neutral finished prototype.

The state serves geometry inspection. A fabrication state describes the SUBJECT's own construction — its plating, seams, frames and open apertures — never the yard equipment around it. The subject is always shown alone.

CAMERA AND COMPOSITION

Choose ONE master view that reveals the most identity-critical geometry. Default for elongated objects: a moderate front three-quarter view. Use a profile view when longitudinal proportion dominates. Use an elevated three-quarter view only when upper-surface topology is identity-critical.

Use normal or moderate-telephoto perspective, low optical distortion, sufficient camera distance and comparable scale between near and far ends. Keep the entire object inside the frame with clear space around every extremity.

State framing in compositional terms only — how the object sits in the frame and how much space surrounds it. Never state pixel dimensions, an aspect ratio, or words such as landscape or portrait. The caller passes the canvas to the API as a separate size parameter; a canvas word inside the prompt fights that parameter and the model resolves the conflict by shrinking the subject.

SCENE AND APPEARANCE

Use a seamless neutral light-grey technical background. State the isolation affirmatively: the subject stands alone, the background field is uniform on every side of it including directly beneath its lowest point, and the subject touches nothing.

Remove people, vehicles, tools, furniture, vegetation, water, dramatic sky, buildings, signage, text, logos, construction clutter and decorative props.

Use soft large-source neutral lighting with controlled contrast and readable shading transitions across the subject's own surfaces, revealing curvature, planes, edges, setbacks, recesses, overhangs and openings. On bare or unfinished metal keep the specular response low and diffuse so the surface reads as matte material rather than polished sheet.

Target photorealistic professional technical reference photography with physically plausible materials and low visual noise. Do not turn the reference into concept art, illustration, blueprint, CAD screenshot, exploded diagram, marketing poster or cinematic advertisement.

GEOMETRIC FAILURE PREVENTION

Identify the likely identity failures for this object and this source: wrong number of levels; extra volumes; missing components; a blunt shape where a sharp termination is required; incorrect symmetry; detached components; random openings; compressed proportions; overly tall massing; incorrect orientation; decorative geometry absent from the canonical design.

Write each one as the COMPLETE STATE THAT MUST BE TRUE INSTEAD, never as a negation, and place it in Constraints. The target model has no negative-prompt channel and treats the words it reads as things to draw: "no blunt bow" puts a blunt bow into the description, while "the bow terminates in a fine edge meeting the waterline at an acute angle" puts the correct shape there.

Reserve the Avoid line for a short list of non-geometric nuisances only — text, logos, watermark, border, image grid. Keep it to one line.
