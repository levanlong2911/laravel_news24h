HOW THIS EXAMPLE WAS DECIDED

Read it against the input beside it. Its subject is a working harbour ferry.
Yours is not. Copy the reasoning, never the vessel, the crews or the
sentences.

WHAT WAS SET ASIDE FROM THE INSPIRATION

Every concrete element of the source ferry was listed first and then
excluded: its length, beam and passenger count, the aluminium catamaran
hull, the enclosed saloon and its bar, and the open upper deck at the
stern. None of them appears in the answer, renamed or otherwise.

Only one abstract question was kept: how can each stop take less of the
crossing? The design was worked out from that question, not from the
source ferry. Its identical ends, its gates along both sides, its benches
across the width and its helm cabin at mid-length each follow from asking
where time is spent at a stop, and none of them is a source feature turned
the other way round.

A proposal that kept the catamaran and the saloon and added side gates was
rejected. It would still be the source ferry with one feature added. So
would a proposal built by reversing what the source does, because a
reversal still starts from the source.

WHAT THE THESIS DECIDED

central_idea states one rule: people move on and off across the ferry's
width. The other four fields are that rule seen
from four sides, which is why they can be checked against each other.

PRINCIPAL DIMENSIONS

Length and beam were chosen after the thesis, inside the profile's bounds,
and neither repeats the source. The rationale explains each number from
the thesis in words and does not restate it. No measurement appears
anywhere else in the answer.

SPATIAL CONSISTENCY

Every place keeps one position for the whole film: the hall runs the full
length under one roof, the gates line both sides, the benches cross the
hall, and the helm cabin sits on the roof at mid-length above the crew
stair. The stair appears in the design stage before anyone uses it, so
nothing arrives from nowhere. Every route in the answer, boarding across
the width or coming down from the helm cabin into the hall, is possible in
that arrangement.

OBSERVATION IS NOT PROOF

Completion shows gates being opened and closed and people stepping through
them. It does not say the gates keep weather out. Operation shows
passengers sitting beside the closed side while wind moves across the
harbour. It does not say the vessel was proven weathertight or stable.

WHAT THE STAGE TREATMENTS ARE FOR

Each one says four things, and they are different questions.

dramatic_purpose is why the stage is in the film at all. Not what happens
in it, but what the film would lose without it.

observable_development is what someone present could see happen. It stays
concrete without becoming a scene: "a crew member runs a hand along the
seams of a closed side" is observable, "the team maintains quality
standards" is not.

participants names who is present, by role and as a group where the work is
collective. A fitting-out crew is one participant, not six workers.

handover_to_next says what carries the film forward. The design entry ends
with the arrangement leaving the studio, and the construction entry begins
from the drawings it produced.

The final stage has nothing after it, so its handover_to_next says what the
film leaves the viewer with instead.

WHAT THIS STEP DOES NOT DO

There are no scenes here, no scene count, no durations, no shot lists, no
dialogue and no instructions about how anything is filmed. The synopsis
tells the film as it unfolds, and the stage treatments say what each
stretch is for. Where the cuts fall is decided later, by someone reading
this.

Five stage treatments appear because the profile lists five stages. That is
the profile's order, not a target for anything else.
