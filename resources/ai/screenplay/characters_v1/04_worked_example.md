HOW THIS EXAMPLE WAS DECIDED

Read it against the input beside it. Its subject is a working harbour ferry.
Yours is not. Copy the reasoning, never the vessel, the crews or the
sentences.

WHAT THE FOUNDATION ALREADY DECIDED

The foundation fixed the ferry: two identical working ends, gates along
both sides, benches across the width, a helm cabin at mid-length above a
crew stair. None of that is chosen again here. The ferry is declared as the
one protagonist, and its appearance repeats what the foundation made
visible.

WHO IS DECLARED

The profile's cast is protagonist_only, so the ferry is the only character.
The design team, the yard crews, the fitting-out crews, the master, the crew
and the passengers are not declared; the scenes describe them in their
action by role and by the work they do.

HOW THE PROFILE WAS WRITTEN

The profile details only what the foundation made: the hall, the two ends,
the side gates, the helm cabin, the cross benches. It adds no room the
foundation does not have. Length and beam are copied from the foundation as
inherited figures; draft, decks, seated passengers and crew are proposed;
speed is left undetermined rather than guessed. The prose names counts but
never a measurement with a unit.

Every position is named on the ferry's own axes: the port and starboard
rows of gates, the helm cabin on the centreline at mid-length, the mooring
stations in the port and starboard corners of each end. Levels are stated
against each other: the hall a short step above the pier, the crew spaces
below it, the helm cabin above the roof. The structure, the passengers' way
on and off and the crew's routes are written apart, and no route is called
the only one. The ends, which stand in for a stern on a ferry with two
working ends, are described in full, including that they have no way down
to the water. What the foundation does not give, such as the bulwarks, the
walkway, the bicycle racks and the finishes, is marked as a proposal.

form_and_proportions starts with the hull itself: its sheer and ends in
profile, its freeboard below the gate tracks, its full width held almost to
each end in plan, and only then the hall standing on its sides and the roof
over it. The foundation gives a low, even body with matching ends; the
freeboard and the plan are marked as proposals. The faces of each part are
named the way they face with the gates closed, and a sentence about what
shows from outside mentions only what faces outward, such as the rails seen
through the glazing.

Three signature features were chosen because each follows from the central
idea, not because they are amenities, and none is a part of another. The
gates and the helm cabin are inherited: the foundation already describes
them. The gates move exactly as the
foundation says: they slide toward one end and stack behind a neighbour,
and the profile only makes that concrete. The folding rail is a proposal,
and it is there because the open side meets the wind and spray named in
premise.force, not to have a moving part; had the foundation not raised
that problem, a fixed design would have been the complete answer.

The profile names the roof as its focus region, so the helm cabin is
written in full: its shape in plan and in profile, its floor against the
roof, the stair and hatch that reach it from the hall, and the door to the
walkway. A focus region gets its own feature with every part of it stated,
not a sentence inside another section.

transformable_spaces names both moving features, the gates and the rails.
Where another section describes how the gates look or what they do, as
windows_and_glazing and wellness_and_relaxation do, it says they are
closed, which is their standard state. Each has one standard state that can exist with the others:
gates closed, cabin installed, rails raised. Every section except
transformable_spaces describes the ferry in those states; the open side and
the lowered rails are written only in transformable_spaces and in each
feature's other_states, so they never reach an anchor image. Each
standard_geometry describes built form only.

THE INTERIOR SPACES

The film_brief lists one space, the passenger hall, so interior_spaces has
one entry, under that key. It keeps the deck and the neighbours the
foundation's space_plan gives it and adds what someone standing in the room
would find: its zones, every way in, its materials and light, what is built
in, and what makes it recognizable. What the foundation does not decide is
marked as a proposal in the prose or named in undetermined, as the hall's
standing capacity is. A brief with more spaces has one entry for each.

The eleven sections still describe the ferry as a whole. spatial_layout
says where the hall lies and how the crew stair joins it to the helm cabin;
the lights under the roof are written only in interior_spaces, so an
exterior image never carries them. The cabin count, the crew stair and the
hatch agree in every place they appear.
