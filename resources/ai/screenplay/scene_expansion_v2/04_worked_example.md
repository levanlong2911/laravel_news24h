HOW THIS EXAMPLE WAS DECIDED

Read it against the input beside it. Its subject is a working harbour ferry.
Yours is not. Copy the reasoning, never the vessel, the crews or the
sentences.

WHAT THE FOUNDATION ALREADY DECIDED

The foundation fixed the ferry: two identical working ends, gates along
both sides, benches across the width, a helm cabin at mid-length above a
crew stair. None of that is chosen again here. Every scene shows part of
it, and no scene adds a part of the ferry the foundation does not name.

The characters and the locations were declared before this step and are
used here by their ids. No scene adds, renames or redescribes one. The
crew stair is not a location: the foundation placed it, and the scenes keep
it where it was.

PEOPLE WITHOUT IDS

The profile's cast is protagonist_only, so the ferry is the only
character. The design team, the yard crews, the fitting-out crews, the
operator representatives, the master, the deck hands and the passengers
are written into the action by role, each with the dress or the things
they carry that make them recognizable, and described the same way
whenever they return. They never appear in character_ids and nobody
speaks. character_ids holds the ferry wherever it is present and is empty
in the design scene, where only a paper strip stands for it.

THE DESIGN ON SCREEN

The ferry carries its profile, and the scenes keep its positions: the
gates are on the port and starboard sides, the helm cabin on the roof at
mid-length. Each signature feature is shown doing something rather than
named: the gates are hung and slid shut, the folding rails are hinged in
place at finishing, folded down by the operator representatives at
completion and by the deck hands at the pier, and raised again before the
ferry leaves.

The side gates are the feature the foundation makes central, so their
whole course stays in the film: the team marks them along the paper strip
in sc_01, the sides rise as openings in sc_02, the gates are hung and slid
shut in sc_03, the operator's people work them in sc_04, a crowd passes
through them in sc_05, they keep the spray out in sc_06, and in sc_07 the
passengers wait at them for the next pier. They always slide toward one
end, as the foundation says, and no scene gives them another way of moving.

Whenever people use the gates the order is the same: they open, people pass
through, everyone is clear of the gate line, and only then do they close.
sc_05 writes the whole order. sc_04 shows only part of it, in the same
order: the gates open, a rail is folded and raised, and they close once
everyone is inside the gate line. Both say who works the gates: the
operator's people at completion, the master from the helm cabin in service.

WHAT EARNS A SCENE

Each scene gives the viewer something the scenes before it did not. Asked
what the film loses without it, every scene has its own answer: the rule of
the width in sc_01, the matching ends and open sides in sc_02, the sides
closing into one surface in sc_03, the arrangement making sense to the
people who will run it in sc_04, a whole crowd boarding at once in sc_05,
shelter under way in sc_06, and the passengers already standing where the
side will open in sc_07.

sc_06 changes nothing physical. It stays because the ending needs a pause
in which the closed side is simply lived beside; a pause that lets a
feeling land earns its place as much as a piece of work.

The gates open and close in sc_04 and again in sc_05, because the
foundation puts both a handover and a boarding in the film. Each keeps only
what its purpose needs. sc_04 is there to show the gates passing to the
people who will run them, so it keeps only that: the operator's own people
open the quay side, work a rail and close it while the fitting-out crews
stand back and the master watches. The foundation also has them step out
onto the quay, try the side facing the water and hand the master his
controls in the helm cabin; the scene leaves those out, and its coverage
evidence claims none of them. sc_05 keeps two moments of the boarding:
the gates opening as the crowd starts across the width, and the gates
closing behind a seated crowd as the ferry pulls away. The
check in sc_03 looks at something else again: a hand running along the
seams of a closed side, with no gate moving. sc_07 ends before the gates
open, so the film never runs the cycle a third time.

HOW STAGE TREATMENTS BECAME SCENES

The design treatment became one scene, because one continuous situation at
one table carries it. The construction treatment became one montage: both
ends, the sides and the roof are one stretch of work in one building hall,
and a separate scene for the ends and another for the sides would only
change which crew is working. Finishing is one montage on the hull afloat.
Operation became three scenes: the boarding at the home pier, then the
crossing and the arrival at the far pier that carry the foundation's
ending. The count follows what each treatment needs, not a quota per stage
and not the coverage list: sc_02 answers two coverage items and sc_03 two
more.

Each handover_to_next reappears as the leads_to of the last scene of its
stage. The last scene realizes the foundation's ending and leads nowhere.

CONTINUOUS SCENES AND MONTAGES

A continuous scene holds only what can happen while the viewer watches: a
team round a table, one stretch of the handover, a crossing. Framing two
ends, raising the sides and roofing a hull take weeks, so sc_02 is a
montage, and so is sc_03, where the gates are hung and the rails and
benches follow over the following days. Each montage stays in one place.

A montage is not only for weeks of work. Opening the side, a whole crowd
boarding and settling, closing and pulling away take minutes, not the few
seconds a scene can give them. sc_05 keeps the two moments that matter, the
side opening as the crowd starts across and the side closing in front of a
full hall, and its b2 says in time_jump that a few minutes have passed with
everyone aboard. The boarding is not squeezed into a continuous scene, and
the duration is estimated from the two moments it keeps.

The beat that follows a gap says in time_jump what was done in the gap, and
never the work that beat goes on to do. In sc_02, b2 begins "Some days
later, with the first end framed", the work b1 showed, and goes on to frame
the second end; b4 begins "Several days later, with every post set" and
goes on to put the roof on. A time_jump of "with the roof on" before a beat
that puts the roof on would show the same work twice.

Each beat is a development of the story, not a shot. "Crews hang the gates
one by one" is one beat, however many shots later show it, and no beat
carries a camera, a framing or a cut.

SUBJECT STATE

The design scene carries null: the ferry is not shown. Every later scene
records the ferry as it opens and as it closes, even when nothing changed,
so continuity can be checked scene by scene. Each start follows the end of
the scene before it; sc_03 starts afloat because the launch is accounted
for between sc_02 and sc_03.

Progress and configuration are kept apart. Progress says what is built.
Configuration says only which parts that open or close a space matter and
what state they are in: the gates are not listed before sc_03 hangs them,
and in sc_04 they open and close during the scene but start and end closed.
The ferry's permanent design is in neither; it comes from its profile.

WORK LEFT OUT, PROGRESS KEPT

The launch is left out between sc_02, the roofed hull still in the building
hall, and sc_03, the first scene with the ferry afloat alongside the
fitting-out quay. Nothing shows it, yet nothing is lost: sc_02 ends with
the hull in the hall, sc_03 starts with it afloat, and coverage names both
scenes. From sc_03 on, every scene has the ferry afloat; none returns it to
the hall or launches it a second time.

LIGHT, WEATHER AND PROPS

Each scene's light comes from the light sources and openings its location
declares: the studio's windows and lamps, the building hall's roof lights
and floodlights, the ferry hall's side openings and, once the gates hang,
their glazing. The light follows the progress, not the finished ferry: in
sc_02 the daylight falls on open framing until the roof goes on and then
reaches inside only through the open bays, and in sc_03 it comes through
the openings still waiting for gates. Weather appears only where the place
is open to the sky or seen through an opening, and the spray of the
crossing belongs to sc_06, not to the hall.

Props are the loose things the action handles: the paper strip and card
benches in sc_01, the template and drawings in sc_02, the passengers' bags
and newspapers later. The benches, gates and rails are never props, because
they are the ferry and its subject state records them; the crane and staging
are never props, because the location fixes them. sc_04 needs no loose
object, so its props are [].

COVERAGE

Every item the profile declares is answered once. Required items point at
the scenes that show them. cov_build_launch is a transition: the move from
the building hall to the water is left out, and the evidence names the
scene before and the scene after.

The film_brief lists the passenger hall, so the profile adds
cov_space_passenger_hall. It is shown by sc_04 and sc_06, both set at
lo_ferry_hall, the location that carries brief_space "passenger_hall":
the operator's people work its gates there, and passengers sit on its
benches. A pier scene outside the hall could not carry it, however much of
the hall it saw. sc_03 is set in the hall too, but it fits the hall out and
nobody uses it yet, so the evidence leaves it out. Engine room work stays
off screen: no scene happens there and nothing in the film turns on it.

THE LOCATIONS SERVE THE SCENES

The scenes were worked out from what happens, and each was then placed
where its action happens. The locations did not set the list.

lo_fitting_quay and lo_far_pier have no scene. The ferry lies alongside the
quay while it is fitted out and comes alongside the far pier at the end,
but in both stretches what the film needs happens inside the hall, so both
places stay unused.

lo_ferry_hall returns four times, each time for something new: the gates
are hung in sc_03, the operator's people work them in sc_04, passengers sit
beside them under way in sc_06, and stand at them as the far pier comes
alongside in sc_07.

Not every movement or operation is a scene. The launch is a transition
between sc_02 and sc_03. In sc_04 the master is already at the foot of the
crew stair; how he came down from the helm cabin is not a scene of its own.

EVERY BEAT WHERE ITS SCENE IS

Every beat happens at its scene's location, and what lies beyond a boundary
is only seen.

sc_03 is set in the hall. The helm cabin on the roof is glazed in the same
days, but that work is named in the time_jump as done in the gap, not
written as a beat in a hall scene.

sc_04 keeps every beat in the hall. The foundation also has the
representatives step out onto the quay and hands the master his controls
in the helm cabin; the scene leaves both out rather than move them into
the hall. A scene may show less than its source, never relocate it.

sc_05 is set at the pier, and its main action is there: the gates slide
open along the pier side and the crowd crosses the width. The master works
the gates from the helm cabin; the scene names who works them without
moving to the cabin.

sc_07 is about the passengers standing at the closed gates, so it is set in
the hall, and the far pier is what they see coming alongside beyond the
glazing. Written from the pier, the scene would have its main action on the
other side of its boundary.

A MOMENT, NOT A ROUND TRIP

The rooms are shown through what happens in them, not through people
arriving and leaving. sc_06 is already out on open water with the
passengers seated; nobody walks in. sc_07 ends as the ferry comes alongside,
before anyone leaves. Arrival is shown only in sc_05, where crossing the
width at once is the point.

ONE SCENE PICKS UP FROM THE LAST

Each scene starts from what the one before left. The crowd that boards with
bags and coffee cups in sc_05 is the crowd seated on the benches in sc_06,
reading the newspapers they carry; in sc_07 the same passengers fold those
papers and stand. The ferry pulls away in sc_05 and is under way in sc_06,
so nothing between them needs explaining.

SCALE IS NOT A TARGET

This ferry needs seven scenes. A larger subject with more coverage needs
more. The inventory below belongs to a different, much larger subject, a
river crossing that carries a road and a railway, whose profile declared
twenty-six coverage items. It lists only what the viewer must understand in
each scene. It is a scale for comparison, not a count to aim for: your
count comes from your foundation, anywhere within the profile's limits.

WHAT THIS STEP DOES NOT DO

There is no logline, design thesis, premise, synopsis, stage treatment or
ending in the answer: the application keeps the foundation's versions.
There are no characters or locations: the application keeps the declared
ones. There is no dialogue, because nobody is declared who could speak. There are no shot lists, no camera instructions and no measurements.
