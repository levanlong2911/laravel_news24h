HOW THIS EXAMPLE WAS DECIDED

Read it against the input beside it. Its subject is a working harbour ferry.
Yours is not. Copy the reasoning, never the vessel, the crews or the
sentences.

WHAT THE FOUNDATION ALREADY DECIDED

The foundation fixed the ferry: two identical working ends, gates along
both sides, benches across the width, a helm cabin at mid-length above a
crew stair. None of that is chosen again here. Every scene shows part of
it, and no scene adds a part of the ferry the foundation does not name.

The characters and the locations were declared before this step and are
used here by their ids. No scene adds, renames or redescribes one. The
crew stair is not a location: the foundation placed it, and the scenes keep
it where it was.

HOW STAGE TREATMENTS BECAME SCENES

The design treatment became one scene, because one continuous situation at
one table carries it. The construction treatment became two, because the
ends and the sides are different work at different moments. Operation
became three, because the ending the foundation describes happens at a
second pier after a crossing. The count follows what each treatment needs,
not a quota per stage.

Each handover_to_next reappears as the leads_to of the last scene of its
stage. The last scene realizes the foundation's ending and leads nowhere.

BUILD STATE

The design scene carries null: nothing of the ferry is built yet. Every
later scene records the ferry as it is at that scene, even when it has not
changed, so continuity can be checked scene by scene.

The launch is left out between sc_03, the roofed hull still in the building
hall, and sc_04, the first scene with the ferry afloat alongside the
fitting-out quay. From sc_04 on, every scene has the ferry afloat; none
returns it to the hall or launches it a second time.

COVERAGE

Every item the profile declares is answered once. Required items point at
the scenes that show them. cov_build_launch is a transition: the move from
the building hall to the water is left out, and the evidence names the
scene before and the scene after.

SCALE IS NOT A TARGET

This ferry needs eight scenes. A larger subject with more coverage needs
more. The inventory below belongs to a different, much larger subject, a
river crossing that carries a road and a railway, whose profile declared
twenty-six coverage items. It lists only what the viewer must understand in
each scene. It is a scale for comparison, not a count to aim for: your
count comes from your foundation and your coverage, anywhere within the
profile's limits.

WHAT THIS STEP DOES NOT DO

There is no logline, design thesis, premise, synopsis, stage treatment or
ending in the answer: the application keeps the foundation's versions.
There are no characters or locations: the application keeps the declared
ones. There are no shot lists, no camera instructions and no measurements.
