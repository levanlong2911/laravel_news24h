HOW THIS EXAMPLE WAS DECIDED

Read it against the input and the output beside it. It shows how scene
boundaries were drawn and how the action was written. It does not stand in
for the design work this assignment asks of you.

WHAT THE THESIS DECIDED

central_idea states one rule: do not screen the gorge. The other four fields
are that rule seen from four sides, which is why they can be checked against
each other.

It does not claim the open deck survives wind better than a solid one. The
input says the deck is open so wind passes through; the thesis repeats that
and stops. A thesis explaining why thin hangers and a shallow cable curve
also follow from wind would be inventing engineering it cannot establish.

WHY DESIGN CAME BEFORE PREMISE

The question, what it is like to cross on something you can see through, is
readable straight off central_idea. It was written after the design, not
before. A premise chosen first would have produced a different vessel fitted
to it.

WHY THE DEVICE HOLDS

The device is a motif, not a prohibition: the walking surface is in frame in
all six scenes. Check a device against every scene before keeping it. One
that is true of three scenes out of six is a claim the film does not keep.

An arbitrary rule, never crossing to the far side for instance, would also
be memorable, but it would not make the design easier to read.

WHERE THE BOUNDARIES FALL

sc_02 holds four actions: a section hangs out of line, a bar goes in, it
levers twice, pins go down the joint. One place, one unbroken stretch of
time, one situation. It is one scene. Splitting it at the second lever would
divide a continuous action for no reason.

sc_03 has no one in it and nothing is done. It is a scene because the deck's
own end and the open gap are a state the film has not shown, and because the
work in sc_04 happens behind that end.

sc_01 to sc_02 skips the sections that went out before this one. sc_03 says
the joints behind the end are weathered, and sc_06 says the rock is bare
where the staging stood. Passing time is shown in the world, not announced.

This film has no CONTINUOUS scene, because no scene begins in the moment the
last one ended. CONTINUOUS is for exactly that, usually across a change of
place that takes no time to make: someone pushes open the hut door, and the
next scene starts as they step out onto the rim. It is not a way to skip a
walk, and it is not a reason to split a scene.

This film has no dialogue either. Nothing said would do work the images do
not already do.

HOW THE COUNT CAME OUT

    the surface is chosen, not inherited          sc_01
    a hanging section becomes part of the run     sc_02
    the deck is a grid with nothing under it      sc_02  sc_03
    the surface and the sides are made continuous sc_04
    the line is unbroken                          sc_05
    the design is used as it was decided          sc_06

Six things, six scenes, but not one each: sc_02 carries two, and one of them
is shared with sc_03. The count is what the list came to. The profile allowed
thirty.

WHAT WAS NOT FILMED

Sections arrive at the open end somehow. The film never shows it. Showing it
would mean describing what carries a section out over a gorge, and nothing in
the material establishes that. The scenes that are here all sit on work whose
support and access are visible: a section already hanging on its hangers, a
rigger kneeling on deck that is already fixed.

Leaving an operation out is allowed. Inventing a mechanism to fill the gap is
not.

A WEAKER ALTERNATIVE

    sc_01  design        An engineer draws a bridge.
    sc_02  construction  Towers rise on both rims.
    sc_03  construction  Cables are spun between them.
    sc_04  finishing     Deck sections are installed.
    sc_05  completion    The bridge is complete.
    sc_06  operation     People cross it.

Every line is true and the order is even correct: towers do come before
cables, and cables before deck. That is not what is missing.

What is missing is any action that belongs to this bridge. Each line names a
stage and reports that it happened. The one decision that makes this crossing
what it is, the open surface, never appears in a single scene, so the film
would be the same film for a solid deck.

It is weak because no scene shows anything specific to the design. Not
because nothing goes wrong.

TRANSFER TO THE REQUESTED SUBJECT

Take the method, not the shapes: decide the design first, let the premise
follow from it, keep each scene to one situation in one place, and write
explanations that say what a scene contributes without claiming more than the
action shows. Do not carry over the gorge, the grid, the scene count or the
absence of dialogue.
