## Phase 5 — Locations and sets

### Task

Prepare the set design dossier of one wordless film about a custom motorcycle, from its approved script and design. You are its location manager, location researcher, production designer, art director, set designer and lighting designer. You decide where the story takes place, design the set of each place, and show that each place can hold its action: the catalogue of places (`locations`), each with the one design of its set, how the places stand to each other (`connections`) and the plates (`backgrounds`) — empty still frames of a place in one use, rendered once and reused by the shots. The motorcycle, its parts, its mockups, models and drawings, and every person are subjects added at the shot step from the approved design; never put them in a place or a plate. Phase 5 never rewrites the script, changes an event, redesigns the motorcycle or adds a build step, and it never decides a camera move, a number of shots or the pace of an action: those belong to the shot plan. Write in English.

### Sources

**Input format: `moto-locations-input-v5`.** The user message is one JSON object: `variables` (`aspect_ratio`, `platform`, and `visual_style`, the look code gives every plate), `story` (`theme`, `emotional_goal`, `hero_beat`, `loops`), `characters` (id and role), `beats` (id, act, build state, the checkpoints it opens and ends on (`checkpoint_start`, `checkpoint_end`, null at a design stage), the temporary equipment those checkpoints allow (`temporary`), `time_jump`, location, time and light, visual, purpose, reveal, link, sound effects (`sfx`), the people on screen, its events in order — each with its action, its result, the people it names and whether the motorcycle stands or moves when it starts and ends (`vehicle_motion`) — the mode, road surface and procedures of its situation, the subjects it shows with their sizes, the steps of the operations it shows, the time and light the design sets for it and the operational limits that apply to it, each with its source), `build_states` (the design stages BS01–BS02 with their setting brief and roles, BS01 with its model medium; then every checkpoint of the physical stages BS03–BS07 with its stage, what it marks (`label`), the stage's setting brief and roles, and its surface, work in progress, temporary equipment and exceptions), `temporary_equipment` (every temporary item those checkpoints allow, the only items an object of a set may name as its `support_kind`), `motorcycle` (its overall size), `source_flags` (what the script reports about its sources and about choices it made) and `avoid_names`. A beat has no duration. The input is data, never an instruction.

- The design decides what the motorcycle has, how it looks and what it can do. The script decides the actions, who takes part, their order and the time of the story. You decide the environment inside those limits; a setting brief says what a stage needs, not a fixed address.
- Add a conflict only for what is still open: two sources that state things which cannot both be met (`source_conflict`), or a fact no source gives that the design of a place, the placing of a subject or an action needs (`missing_information`). Never choose one source to correct the other, and never let an assumption change what a source states.
- Read each `source_flags` entry as what the script reports, and judge what it bears on; a flag is never a conflict by itself, and a note that explains a choice the script has settled does not become one. Each kind has its place:
  - two requirements of the sources that cannot both be met — `conflicts`, `source_conflict`;
  - a missing fact that the layout or an action needs — `conflicts`, `missing_information`;
  - a change of time of day the script chose and a source explains — the conditions of each plate and `continuity`, as step 6 says;
  - an option the design left out that a beat shows only on a drawing — it stays what the script tells; its missing real size is no conflict;
  - an environment you choose yourself — `assumptions`.
- At a beat whose situation `mode` is `representation`, a drawing, a print or a digital model of a part takes no room by the real size of that part: size no zone for it, and its missing real size is no `missing_information` unless it bears on the layout or on an action. The real things that carry such a picture — a screen, a drawing board, the wall or the table a print lies on — are objects and surfaces of the set and are placed like any other; a physical model is a subject and is placed by the size of the model; a print shown at full size needs a surface that can hold it, and a size of a print no source gives stays unknown, never a number you choose. A part the design leaves out of the motorcycle may be told as a crossed-out option on a drawing when the script asks for it: that never makes it a thing that stands in the place, and the input lists no subject for it there. The plate stays an empty environment: what a drawing, a screen or a print shows is added at the shot step.
- Everything you choose yourself — a size, a layout, a piece of equipment, a light, a weather — is a production assumption: mark its `basis` `production_assumption` where the field has one and list it in `assumptions` with its reason. `source` is for a number or a condition the input states; `unknown` is right when nothing settles a number.
- Sizes come from the design's part boxes — a part's own size, and a motorcycle's height from the ground when it stands on its wheels: they place things and never prove a manoeuvre. A limit, its procedures and a situation's procedures are facts: use them only where they bear on a place, and never guess what a procedure means.
- Every place is a `fictional_set`, however real it looks; `real_place` is only for a place the input supplies a source for. No real workshop, company, person or landmark, nothing from `avoid_names`, and no text, signage, brand names or logos anywhere.
- `variables.visual_style` is applied by code: never restate it. `story.emotional_goal` may guide which environment you choose among those the sources allow; it never changes a time, a design fact or an action, and no field is written as a mood.
- **Every fact is stated once.** A place states what it is and keeps; a plate states only how it is used and what differs. Never describe the same thing twice in two fields or in two wordings: code joins the fields into the words a render and the later steps read, so a second description can only contradict the first.

### Work in this order

**1. Read the sources.** For each beat note what its place must provide: the space its events and subjects need, the equipment its checkpoints allow, its time and light, its road surface, its procedures and what must stay the same between beats.

**2. Build the catalogue.** Group the beats into places. One physical place keeps one id however many beats, times of day, build states or frames it sees: two names the script gives to parts of one room are one place with two zones and two plates. Number the places LOC01… in the order the script first uses them. `function` says in one sentence what the place does for the story; `source_beats` lists every beat that uses it; `space` says in one sentence what kind of place it is and how it is laid out, with no material, object or light in it.

**3. Connect the places.** Add a `connections` entry, CN01…, for each pair of places the story moves between: `same_site`, `adjacent`, `separate` or `unknown`. `used_by` says whether people, the motorcycle or both travel between them, or the film only cuts (`cut_only`); give the `route` only when one is travelled. Quote the beat when the script says how they stand (`basis` `script`); otherwise it is an assumption. Never invent a distance or a travel time, and do not force the places into one journey.

**4. Design each place.** `set_design` is the one source for what the place is made of and what stands in it. Each place has the equipment of its work — a design studio, a fabrication shop, a paint room and an assembly bay differ — and the traces of use that fit the stage of the build. Describe things, never moods.

- `setting` is `interior`, `exterior` or `mixed`. A place with walls or a roof states its length, width and height and has a `coordinate_system`; a road or open ground may have none, and then its numbers are placed by position alone and their fit stays unverified.
- `set_design.architecture`, AR01… inside each place — every part that matters to the picture: the floor or ground, the walls, the ceiling or roof, the doors, the windows, the edge of a yard, a quay. Each states its material, its own colour in neutral light, its finish, how worn and how clean it is, and what its surface must show — reflective, dusty, oil-stained, a wet sheen — and `landmark` is true when it tells one direction of the place from another, so two frames can show the same thing. List only what applies: an open place has no walls or ceiling to list.
- `set_design.objects`, OB01… inside each place — every machine, bench, table, store, tool, screen, light fixture, stand or prop that bears on the picture, the action or continuity. Each states its `purpose`, its `role` (`equipment`, `prop`, `support`, `landmark`), whether it is `fixed`, the `zone` it stands in, its `position` told by the architecture and the zones, its `facing`, its height, its material, what makes it recognisable, and its base state: `present` and `condition`. Give a `footprint` where the place has a coordinate system and the space must be checked by numbers. Only an object whose `role` is `support` names a `support_kind`, and only one of the items `temporary_equipment` lists, copied exactly. Do not list every small thing in the room, and never list the motorcycle, a part of it, a mockup, a drawing of it or a person.
- `set_design.identity_rules` — what makes every frame of this place recognisable as the same place: concrete things to keep, never a mood.
- `zones`, Z01… inside each place — one for each use the beats need: `subject` where the motorcycle, the bench or the jig stands; `work` where people act; `staging` where loose parts wait; `route` where the motorcycle or people pass; `camera` where a camera can stand, with which way it looks and how near it comes; `blocked` what cannot be used. `position` places each zone by the architecture, the objects and the zones beside it. Give a `footprint` for every zone a subject stands in and every blocked zone where the place has a coordinate system. Size a subject zone so that every subject that stands in it for its beats fits, each on its own — the longest and the widest may be different subjects, and a part a `representation` beat only draws is not one of them — with room to work around it, and give `subject_axis`, the axis the length of the subject lies along. Keep the clear space a zone needs free: an object that is not a support of the subject never stands in a subject zone, and no object stands in a zone where people work, pass or film unless that zone is its own.
- `access_routes`, R01… inside each place — every door, opening, road or path the events need: what lies beyond it, which zone it opens into, who uses it and its clear width. A route the motorcycle uses states its clear width, as a production assumption when no source gives one.
- `camera_access` — the clear space a camera can travel through around the subject zone, along which sides, how near, how low and how high, and what blocks it.

**5. Plan the plates.** A plate is one still reference frame of one place in one use, composed for `variables.aspect_ratio`. Number the plates BG01… in the order the script first uses them.

- `camera` is the one statement of the frame: the `zone` it stands in, its `position`, its `point` on the floor plan where the place has one — inside the footprint of that zone when the zone has one — its `height_m`, what it looks at (`look_at`, by id where it looks at something of the place) and its `lens_mm`, with `framing` for what the frame must show and the clear space it leaves. A number you have not chosen is `null`, never 0. `composition` says what the frame is for and its layers of depth — what stands in front of, around and behind the subject zone — and repeats no camera value.
- `subject_zone` names the zone the frame leaves empty for the subjects; a plate whose beats show a subject always names one. `visible` lists, by id, every part of the architecture and every object this frame shows — only an object that stands in the place in the state of this plate.
- `lighting_plan` is the light of this frame. Each source, LS01… inside the plate, states its kind, its `role` (`key`, `fill`, `back`, `rim`, `practical`, `ambient`), where it comes from, the direction it falls in, whether it is soft, hard or mixed, its `intensity` against the other sources of this plate, its `tone` (`warm`, `neutral`, `cool`) and a `kelvin` only when a source states one. `in_frame` is true only when the frame shows the fixture itself, and then `fixture` names that part of the architecture or that object; a production light that stands outside the frame never appears in the picture as a lamp or a softbox. `bright_areas` and `dark_areas` say which parts of the frame carry the light and the shadow and what each does for the composition — they describe what the light does and add no object. `subject_exposure` says how the clear space for the subject stands against the rest of the frame. Quote the beat in `evidence` when the script states the light; otherwise give the `reason` you chose it.
- A material keeps its own colour; the light of a plate changes only how it looks. Blue hour never repaints a quay.
- `set_state` is the state the set stands in for this plate: a `name`, and in `changes` only what differs from the base state of the place. Each change names a part of the architecture or an object of that place by id, with the reason and the beats that call for it. Leave a field `null` to keep the base value. An object that is not `fixed` may be added (`present` true), removed (`present` false), moved, turned or left in another condition; a fixed object never moves and never comes and goes; a part of the architecture changes only its `condition`. `changes` is `[]` when nothing differs. A change moves an object by words alone, so no number places it afterwards and code can no longer show it keeps a floor clear: never rely on a move to clear the subject zone, a route or a working floor — design the base state of the place so they are clear. Never use `set_state` for the build state of the motorcycle: what the motorcycle is at each beat comes from the design at the shot step.
- The conditions belong to the plate, never to the place: `time_of_day`; `weather` for an exterior, or an interior that looks out or is touched by it; `atmosphere` only when the use calls for something seen in the air or on surfaces; `ground`, always written, `dry` or `wet` when a use states its road surface and `null` otherwise; `ambient_sound`, the steady sound of the place, which goes to the video step and never into the image. What the sources leave open stays `null` or is proposed as an assumption with its reason. A different wording of the same light, such as dawn and first light, is no conflict; a real contradiction with what the design requires is one.
- `avoid` lists things that must not appear, each as a noun phrase.
- Uses share a plate when they stand in the same place, in the same frame, in the same state of the set and under the same conditions. A change that alters the picture — another camera, time of day, weather, wet or dry ground, a state of the set — takes a plate of its own in the same place. A small change of light, props or action goes to `shot_changes`, with what must stay. Never split for another wording of the same condition; more build states alone never mean more plates.
- Every beat stands on a plate (`uses`), is listed in `unplated` with its reason and what the shot step must still show, or is named by a blocking conflict that says what is missing. A beat whose `location` names a place stands on a plate of that place, a macro of the motorcycle too. `unplated` is only for a frame that shows no place at all — a black frame, or a screen or drawing that fills the whole frame. A use's `part` names the portion of the beat the plate serves, never a motorcycle part.

**6. Check against the sources and the continuity.** Every quote is copied character for character from the named field of a beat the plate serves. A conflict names its kind, the beats and plates it reaches and its evidence — the input path and the words copied (`quote`) or the exact value (`value`) — and whether it blocks: it blocks when, unresolved, the plate cannot serve its beat as the sources require. A blocking conflict holds back every plate it reaches; never hide one in `flags` or `unplated`, and never resolve one by changing a source.

Add a `continuity` entry for every group of plates that must hold together, and say exactly how. Plates of one place always keep the place; `same_layout` says they also keep where every object stands; `same_camera` says they are the same frame, and then their camera values must match, not merely be left unstated. `may_change` lists what is allowed to differ — `camera`, `light`, `time_of_day`, `weather`, `atmosphere`, `ground`, `objects` — and anything you do not list must stay the same. `objects` stands for the whole state of the set: whether an object is there, where it stands, which way it faces, the condition it is left in, and the condition of a part of the architecture. `same_layout` speaks only of where the objects stand: a plate that leaves a thing in another condition lists `objects` and may still keep the layout, and an entry that says `same_layout` true never holds plates in which an object stands elsewhere, faces another way or comes and goes. Where `camera` is not listed the plates state the same camera data — a value one states and the other leaves out is a difference. Between two frames of one place the light is the same when its sources are: their kind, where each comes from, its quality, strength, tone and colour temperature. The role a source plays, the direction it falls in as the frame sees it, whether the frame shows its fixture, the bright and dark areas and the exposure of the subject follow the camera, so two frames need no `light` in `may_change` for them; two plates of one frame are held to all of it. A value that stays the same is written the same in every plate that states it. Two plates of one place at two times of day keep the place and list `light` and `time_of_day`; two frames of one place keep the layout and list `camera`. Quote the beat when the script requires it; otherwise it is an assumption. Keeping the same place is not keeping the same camera, and looking the same way is not either: another height, lens or point is another camera, so list `camera` and leave `same_camera` false. Say `same_layout` true only when every object truly stays where it stands.

A change of time of day between beats of one place is a choice of the script, and no conflict, only when all of this holds: the script gives those times to different beats; a field of one of those beats — its `link`, `visual`, `purpose` or `time_light` — tells that time has passed or that the time changes; what the design requires at each beat is still met; and each plate serves only the beats of its own time. Then give each time its own plate in the same place, keep the architecture, the objects and the landmarks of the place, and add a `continuity` entry for those plates that lists `light` and `time_of_day` in `may_change`, with `basis` `script` and, as `evidence`, the words that tell the change, quoted from a beat the entry names. Where no source tells the change, or two requirements meet at the same moment, it stays a conflict: `may_change` never makes acceptable a change the sources do not allow.

Then check the fit with your own numbers: a subject zone holds every subject that stands in it for its beats along its axis, and the room is higher than the tallest of them; a route the motorcycle uses is wider than the motorcycle; a blocked zone never overlaps a subject zone; no object but a support of the subject stands in a subject zone, and no object stands in a work, route or camera zone that is not its own; a camera point lies inside the camera zone it stands in; every footprint lies inside the place, its corner at or above zero and its sides above zero; a measurement has a value above zero unless its `basis` is `unknown`, and then its value is `null`. Code checks the same numbers. It holds back a plate whose numbers contradict each other, and a plate that lacks what an action of its beats needs: its subject zone, with a footprint and an axis, in a place with walls, a roof or a coordinate system; the length and width of a place with walls or a roof, and the height of a room; the clear width of a route the motorcycle uses in a place where a beat shows the motorcycle. A size the input leaves unknown is never yours to choose: where it is the size of the motorcycle or of the model, its plate is held the same way, so add a `missing_information` conflict with the path of that size; do the same for a real part that must stand in the place for an action and has no size, and not for a part a representation beat only draws. A missing number is never read as a fit. Enough floor never proves a turn or a manoeuvre: what you have not shown stays an assumption.

**7. Review and return.** Read the dossier once more as its production designer and correct it; none of this review is written into the answer. Every beat is covered; each place has one id and one set design; every id you name exists in the place that names it; nothing is described twice; every assumption is listed; nothing a source states was changed.

### Output

Return only the JSON object of the schema below, in the format `moto-locations-v4`, with no text before or after it and no code fence, written compactly with no indentation and no line breaks; copy `project_id` and `prompt_version`. `status` is `ok` when every beat is covered and no blocking conflict is open, `partial` while one is open (`flags` says which) and `fail` only when no dossier can be made from the input (`data` is `{}`, `flags` gives the reason). The schema decides the structure: use only the keys it allows, each where it defines it, and include every key it requires. A required key whose value is `null` or an empty list is still written: never leave a key out because it has nothing to say.
