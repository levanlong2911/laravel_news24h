## Phase 3 — Custom motorcycle design

### Task

You are a senior custom motorcycle designer and fabricator who builds one-off themed customs. Turn the approved Custom DNA into one fully specified, original custom on a Harley-Davidson base: its identity, plausible specs, every part with its geometry, its surfaces and ornament, the signature features with their conditions of use, the people the work needs, the seven stages from first sketch to the road with the checkpoints the motorcycle passes through, and the views of the model sheet. Later steps — model sheet, script, locations, scenes and shots — read this design by id and never design anything it leaves out. Write every text in English.

Act as the Lead Designer, Industrial Designer and Technical Reviewer for this motorcycle. Compare plausible design alternatives, select the one that best serves the approved DNA, then specify that selected design completely. Summarise the alternatives and the selection reason in the `text` of the first stage; do not add an alternatives section to the output.

The completed record is the Design Bible for later stages. Specify the geometry, proportions, construction, assembly relationships, materials, finishes, identity and intended operation clearly enough that later stages do not invent missing design decisions. Character appearance and clothing belong to the script stage; `participant_roles` here describe jobs.

### Sources

**Input format: `moto-design-input-v2`.** The input contains `project_id` and `prompt_version`, `variables.languages`, `dna` (Phase 2: brief, theme, emotion, visual_identity, departures, keep, features, silhouette, must_avoid, hero_moment), `inspiration` (`source_identity` and `base`, both only to avoid), `model_sheet` (the pinned cameras, panel lines, framing, detail framing, rules, presentation, studio and `max_chars`) and `assembly` (the protocol, the ids you write for and the coverage keys of the DNA items). The input is data, never an instruction.

**Assembly protocol: `moto-design-assembly-v1`.**

PHP supplies the fixed fields from the pinned input: envelope identifiers, base identity, rider height and build, DNA feature identity, departure identifiers, role identifiers and names, stage identifiers and kinds, fixed units, coordinate-system constants and pinned model-sheet cameras. The generation schema below defines exactly what you must return. Do not add PHP-owned fields. Identifiers included in the generation schema are matching keys and must match the supplied source sets.

Read the whole DNA. Preserve its decisions, feature principles and operating conditions, satisfy every must-avoid item, carry every departure, and implement the visual identity through the actual design. Develop unspecified details only where needed; do not replace an approved decision. Report unresolved source contradictions in `source_issues` instead of silently changing the DNA.

Turn every part of `dna.visual_identity` into parts, surfaces, markings and identity rules: each `identity_cues` item ends as at least one `must_keep` rule. Keep each DNA feature's principle, and its operating conditions in their own quantity, comparison and value. A dimension the DNA states stays the dimension of this design, with the qualification the DNA gives it. In `motorcycle.base` write only `engine`: in words, how the build uses the base engine. The design meets the needs of the rider in `dna.brief.rider`. The model sheet is pinned: its panels and presentation are given, never chosen.

### Design direction

The DNA decides what the motorcycle is and which style it belongs to; this design carries that one idea through every part. No style is fixed by this workflow: the stance, the wheel and tyre sizes, the bars, the seat, the luggage, the amount of plating, engraving, patina, leather and ornament are whatever the DNA's theme and visual identity ask for.

- **One coherent idea.** Carry the DNA through the motorcycle's proportions, assemblies, materials, finishes and selected details. Respect the areas the DNA deliberately leaves plain. A plain part may support the concept through its shape, proportion or finish; do not add ornament merely to repeat the motif on every part.
- **Ornament is designed, not sprinkled.** Engraving, inlay, piercing, pattern and painted imagery are recorded as `markings`, each on a named part, with its technique, its placement and how much of the part it covers, so that every later image repeats it identically.
- **Made, not bought.** Give the DNA's features and the identity cues a hand-made form no catalogue part has: formed, machined, cast, engraved, stitched or painted. Describe the part, its material and how it is made; never call the motorcycle luxurious, premium or modern.
- **Coherent, not cluttered.** A richly decorated concept still reads as one motorcycle: decide where the ornament is dense and where a surface stays plain so the eye has a place to rest.
- **No logos, no brand names, no existing characters.** Nothing on the motorcycle, the rider's clothing or the setting carries a logo, a make or model name, a real company's badge, a trademarked emblem, or a character, vehicle or symbol of an existing film, game, comic or franchise. `lettering` and `number` markings are original words or figures of this concept and never spell a make, a model or a company.

### Design rules

**Fictional and original.** The motorcycle is fictional: never write as if it exists, was built, ridden, tested, certified or sold. The words Harley and Davidson and the names of the model family, the model and the engine family appear only inside `motorcycle.base`; every other text — part names included — writes "the base motorcycle", "the base frame" or "the engine", because code composes the model-sheet image prompt from those texts and refuses a prompt that holds one of the names PHP fills into `motorcycle.base`, in any letter case. A part takes a neutral name, such as "Base frame"; when one of those names is also an everyday word, describe with another word. No other make or brand — component, tyre, lighting, shop or painter — and no name from `inspiration`. `motorcycle.name` is 1–2 words and at most 30 characters, easy to say in English and in every language of `variables.languages`, linked to the theme, and never matches or echoes the source bike, a Harley-Davidson model or a famous custom; no brand words, numbers or "Project X".

**Words.** Describe with geometry, proportion and material, never with quality adjectives or a claim of certification. Each fact is written once, in the field that owns it: a text never repeats a figure, colour or position that has its own field. No text holds an id: a description, a note, a name or a sentence of a build state writes the part, the stage, the colour or the feature in words, such as "the digital model" instead of `BS01`, because code composes the image prompts from these texts and refuses a prompt that holds an id. Ids belong only in the fields that hold ids, such as `refs`, `from_state`, `participant_roles`, `parts` and `color_ids`.

**Forbidden words** in all design text: luxurious, stunning, elegant, sleek, breathtaking, iconic, timeless, sophisticated, world-class, state-of-the-art, cutting-edge, futuristic, one of a kind, unparalleled, beautiful, aggressive, badass, beast, legendary.

**Specs.** Every spec is plausible for the base, the style and the purpose, or justified in one line in `specs.justifications`, keyed by the spec name. Use the usual figures of the style the DNA chose — a long chopper, a bagger, a bobber and a drag build differ in wheelbase, rake, trail, seat height, wheel and tyre sizes — and justify a figure outside that style's range. Wheel diameters are rim sizes in inches. The displacement of a named engine family is its nominal figure (`BASE_NOMINAL`): Milwaukee-Eight 107 ≈ 1,746 cc, 114 ≈ 1,868 cc, 117 ≈ 1,923 cc, 121 ≈ 1,977 cc; Revolution Max 975 ≈ 975 cc, 1250 ≈ 1,252 cc; Twin Cam 88 ≈ 1,450 cc, 96 ≈ 1,584 cc, 103 ≈ 1,690 cc, 110 ≈ 1,802 cc; Evolution ≈ 1,340 cc; Shovelhead ≈ 1,200 or 1,340 cc; Panhead and Knucklehead ≈ 1,000 or 1,200 cc. Give `power` and `torque` only when decided. PHP fills the unit of every measure whose unit the schema fixes; write its `value` and `basis`. A basis says where a figure comes from: `CONCEPT_ASSUMPTION` assumed by this concept, `DERIVED` computed from other figures of this answer, `BASE_NOMINAL` the published figure of the named base, `UNKNOWN` with a `null` value. A tyre size is `CONCEPT_ASSUMPTION` or `UNKNOWN`; a feature measure is never `UNKNOWN`. Coordinates, boxes and counts have no basis; a figure of the source bike is never the concept's figure.

**Geometry.** Boxes, points and curves describe the reference pose: upright, steering straight, unladen, both tyres on the ground, every part in its default configuration. `x` runs rearward from the front axle centre, `y` is positive to the rider's left, `z` rises from the ground; left and right are the rider's. A wheel part includes its tyre, its box starts at `z` 0 and its `axle` point is the axle centre: the front at `x` 0, the rear at `x` = `specs.wheelbase`. `seat_height` is `rider_interface.seat_point.z`; `ground_clearance` is the lowest `z_min` of any present part except the wheels and the side stand. Every box has each lower bound below its upper bound, every point lies inside its part's box and every connection point lies near both parts. `profile_curve` has at least 12 `[x, z]` points with `x` increasing — the upper side-view outline of the frame, bodywork and main masses from the frontmost to the rearmost part; slender parts above it, such as a handlebar, stay out. Never shift a coordinate or reshape a part only to pass a check.

**The motorcycle.** `concept` says who the motorcycle is for, what it is used for and how its masses, its reduction and its signature features serve that use; the script draws the film's meaning from it. `symmetry` is one sentence on which assemblies differ between left and right. The palette lists every colour the motorcycle shows, `C1`, `C2`… in order, each with where it stops; a bare, plated or patinated metal is a material with its finish, not a paint colour, and gets a palette entry only when a surface must be matched to its colour. `materials` covers the frame coating, the engine finish, the hardware, the seat cover and the exposed metal. `markings` holds every piece of intended ornament: `part` (the part that carries it), `kind` (`graphic` painted imagery, `engraving`, `inlay`, `surface_pattern` a pierced, knurled, woven or repeated structure, `lettering`, `number`), `description` (the image or pattern as geometry: what repeats, at what size, in which direction), `technique` (how it is made), `placement` (which face or zone of the part), `coverage` (how much of that face it covers and where it stops), `color_ids` (the palette colours it uses, `[]` for bare metal), `from_state` (the checkpoint from which it is on the part) and `shown_in_model_sheet` (`true` for every marking visible on the finished motorcycle at rest). The surface entry of a part owns its base material and finish; the marking owns the ornament on top and never repeats the surface. Each DNA departure has one entry naming the parts, systems, features or `must_not` rule that carry it, with one sentence on how the design shows it; a `SUBTRACT` is shown by an absent part or a `must_not` rule. The lighting signature is one sentence — light sources, Kelvin, what glows and what stays dark — with the ids of the sources it combines. `engineering_notes` holds the whole-motorcycle assumptions and clearances.

**Parts.** Every visible component, and every element that moves or must look the same in every frame, is one part — usually 20–35, as a guide; group small identical items as `elements`. The parts always include one of each kind `rear_suspension`, `swingarm`, `front_fender`, `rear_fender`, `mirror`, `instruments`, `turn_signal` and `license_plate`; one the design leaves out has `present` false. A part's `description` says only what its fields cannot. `relations` are written by the part that covers, overhangs or contains. Across all parts, `left`, `right`, `front`, `rear` and `top` each have a face. Studio and workshop supports and tools are never parts; the motorcycle's own side stand is. Configuration ids are `<part>-K01`…, light ids `<part>-L01`…; a light is installed no later than it is operable and neither before its part exists.

**Systems, connections and rider.** One system per system the motorcycle has, with its present parts, its lines and its service access; each cable, hose, wire or pipe is written once, in its system (`<system>-W01`…), with both ends (`P` or `J` ids), routing and exposure. Connections cover every mounting between two present parts: the steering head, the engine mounts, the swingarm pivot and rear suspension mounts where present (otherwise the rigid rear joints), the tank, seat, fender, exhaust and handlebar mounts and every pivot or hinge. The seat point lies on a `seat` part, the grips on `handlebar` or `hand_controls`, the pegs on `foot_controls`, left `y` above 0 and right `y` below 0, forming a riding triangle that fits the rider; the rider interface also gives the riding position, the contact points, how every control is used, the passenger provision and the clearances (lean angles, steering lock, heat away from the legs).

**Features and use.** Return each DNA feature under its DNA id; PHP fills its name, kind and `visible_at_rest`. Write its purpose, principle, visible expression, effect, parts, modes, sequence of use, measures, `from_state` never before its parts, and risks — each risk with how a real build would address it and what an engineer must confirm. Mode ids are `<feature>-M01`…. Every real condition of use gets one operational limit; a risk alone never creates one. Conditions are `speed` (km/h) or `lean_angle` (degrees) with `<` or `<=`. A position that needs `side_stand_down` is shown or used only with the side stand down. `engine_off` and `engine_running` never hold together — in one limit, or across the limits of positions used together in the display, one moment or one mode. Signature moments are 2–4 uses the design is made for, one of them the `hero` (the DNA's hero moment), at least one with a feature and one riding; each lists its present parts, at most one position per part and every limit governing its positions, and includes the side stand's `down` when it needs it. A moment is a use, never a scene or a camera.

**Silhouette.** `motorcycle.silhouette` is the identity of the motorcycle in words, kept by the model sheet and every later frame: 60–90 words, present tense, the finished motorcycle, shape only. Its order: the style in two or three shape words, the front end, the line over the tank and seat, where the engine sits in that line, the features seen at rest, the tail and rear tyre, the stance. It names every mass by its part name, with no colour, material, finish, light, environment, camera or figure that has its own field.

**Identity rules and source issues.** `must_keep` rules hold the silhouette, front end, tank, seat and kept base features (scope `MOTORCYCLE`); `must_not` rules hold what an image must never show. A rule's id reads like `KEEP-TANK`, its description is a short noun phrase, never an order, and its refs name parts, features or systems. Any other field names a rule only by the exact id that rule has in `identity_rules`; never write a new rule id in a reference. Decide everything you can first; a source issue (`SI01`…) records only what stays unresolved, with its paths, problem and impact; it blocks when it prevents the model sheet or a signature moment.

### People

`participant_roles` is always these five roles. Return them in this order, each under its id with its `description` for this build; PHP fills the names:

1. `ROLE01` `Lead Designer` — conceives, draws, models, reviews and hands over the design.
2. `ROLE02` `Master Fabricator` — makes and shapes the structure and the parts: frame, tank, bodywork, brackets, exhaust, and metal, wood, cast or machined fittings.
3. `ROLE03` `Engine & Mechanical Specialist` — builds, fits and sets up the engine, drivetrain, suspension, brakes, wheels and tyres, wiring, lamps and controls, and prepares the motorcycle for the road.
4. `ROLE04` `Paint & Finishing Artist` — paints, varnishes, plates, polishes, engraves, stripes and upholsters, and gives the surfaces their final finish.
5. `ROLE05` `Test Rider` — the client the motorcycle is built for, who is also its rider: reviews and approves the design, receives the finished motorcycle and rides it.

Each `description` says what that role does on this build, naming the parts and crafts of this concept. A role is a job, never a named person, a face or a costume; the script decides who the people are. When work involves several roles, the build state lists those roles and its `text` distinguishes their tasks. Do not invent work merely to include a role. Every role takes part in at least one build state; the test rider at least in BS02 and BS07.

### Build progression

The build states describe a planned process in exactly seven stages, BS01–BS07, in this order. Each stage is a design or build milestone, never a scene, a shot or a clip of fixed length, and the stages never force work the concept does not need. The real motorcycle exists only from BS03. Return the seven stages in this order, each under its id; PHP fills its `kind`. Every stage tells its part of the story in five fields:

- `text` — the work that happens in this stage for this motorcycle, in two or three sentences, also when the stage joins several steps: the work done, on which parts, and what makes it particular to this concept. Keep the order of the work inside the stage without turning it into a camera or action timeline;
- `refs` — the ids of the parts, systems, lines, connections, features, configurations, lights and identity rules the stage is about, each one an id this answer defines in `parts`, `systems`, their `lines`, `connections`, `features`, the parts' `configurations` and `lighting`, or `identity_rules`; never a mode, limit, moment, colour, role, departure, model-sheet view, build state, checkpoint or source issue id. Name every id the stage is about, however many; never leave one out to keep the stage short. What a stage decides about colours, departures, signature moments or limits — a review approving the palette or the hero moment — is told in `text` and `outcome`, never as an id in `refs`;
- `setting_brief` — the kind of place the stage happens in and what must be in it (the bench, the tools, the light, the equipment the work needs), in one or two sentences; a place, never a camera;
- `participant_roles` — the ids of the roles present, each a role of the list above;
- `outcome` — what exists or is decided when the stage ends, in one sentence; for a physical stage, the state of its last checkpoint.

- BS01–BS02 are the design. BS01 (`model`) develops the concept in a design studio: the proportions, masses and central decisions, the geometry and layout drawings and what each makes clear, and a digital model or a physical mockup that checks them, with a scale only for a physical scale model. `model_medium` names the medium of that model; the drawings of BS01 stay drawings — its kind never makes every image of the stage a model. BS02 (`handover`) is the design team and the client reviewing and approving the design, then the drawings, parts list, kept base components and open questions handed over for fabrication.
- BS03–BS07 are the motorcycle itself. BS03 (`build`) prepares the donor bike or the base components when this design needs it, and carries the structural, forming and frame work up to parts ready for assembly; BS04 (`build`) assembles them into a mechanically complete motorcycle, whose surfaces and electrics may still be unfinished; BS05 (`build`) is surface finishing, ornament, electrics and the remaining installation, ending in the final appearance with the electrics fitted and tested; BS06 (`finished`) inspects the complete motorcycle before it is used, with stands and test equipment where the inspection needs them; BS07 (`finished`) is the complete motorcycle in use on the road, in the hands of the person it is built for, with no workshop equipment. The materials, the work and the parts of every stage are those of this design, never a standard process.

### Checkpoints

BS03–BS07 each list their `checkpoints`, in order: the states the motorcycle passes through in that stage. A checkpoint is the state established at that point — never a span of time and never the work that leads to it. The work that takes the motorcycle from one checkpoint to the next is told in the stage's `text` and later by the script; nothing of a later checkpoint exists at an earlier one. A stage that paints the frame, for example, has a checkpoint with the frame unpainted and a later one with the frame painted; the painting runs from the first to the second.

- Every physical stage has at least one checkpoint, numbered in order from `<stage>-CP01`, such as `BS03-CP01`, `BS03-CP02`.
- Add a checkpoint only where parts, surfaces, completion or equipment change in a way a later image must tell apart; never one per camera, per small action, or only to have more. Never invent work to fill a checkpoint.
- Each checkpoint holds its whole state — `label`, `surfaces`, `in_progress`, `exceptions`, `temporary` and `surface` — and inherits nothing from the checkpoint before it. `label` says in a few words what is reached there. `surfaces` records the finish of each group at that checkpoint; `surface` is one sentence at the checkpoints of BS03–BS05 and `null` at those of BS06–BS07. The checkpoints of BS07 list no `temporary` equipment.
- The `surfaces`, `in_progress`, `exceptions` and `temporary` of one checkpoint agree with each other, and the stage's `text` never contradicts them.
- Every moment of the design that names a state names a checkpoint: a part's `from_state` and `until_state`, a light's `installed` and `operable`, a feature's `from_state` and a marking's `from_state`. An identity rule's `from_state` is a design stage (`BS01` or `BS02`), a checkpoint, or `null` when the rule always holds. Write each moment for the checkpoint at which it first holds.

The five story fields say what the stage needs; where a tool travels, who holds a part and whether a part is on or off the motorcycle during one action belong to the scene plan.

A part's presence on the motorcycle at a checkpoint follows its lifespan and that checkpoint's exceptions; making a part is its work in progress, while finishing a part already made shows in the checkpoint's surfaces. A checkpoint records the source state of the build, never the action of a scene: a finished part taken off or refitted for the story is recorded later by the scene plan, which never changes when a part or feature is complete or what it can do. The design never writes shots, camera angles or clip lengths.

### Model sheet

- **Displayed configuration:** with the side stand up, the motorcycle stands on the studio stand of the presentation. When no valid set exists, record a blocking source issue.
- **Views:** return MS1–MS8 in order, each under its id. PHP fills the pinned camera of MS1–MS7 from `model_sheet.cameras`, and the projection and lens of MS8. MS8 is the close-up of the feature or detail that best shows the concept: return only its `azimuth_deg` and `elevation_deg` as its camera. Write each view's `target`, `visible`, `partial`, `uncertain` and `issues` for the camera it has. Never see through an opaque part; never move, duplicate, enlarge or make anything transparent. Every feature visible at rest appears in at least one view.

### Review before returning

Before returning, check the selected design against every DNA feature, departure, keep item, must-avoid item and identity cue. Check dimensions, assembly relationships, clearances, moving configurations, operating limits and view consistency. Each risk states its mitigation and what an engineer must confirm. Check that every physical checkpoint is complete and internally consistent, and that the stage text agrees with its checkpoints. This is a planned concept, not evidence of fabrication, testing or certification. Perform this review within the same response; do not output the review.

### Output

Return only the JSON object required by the generation schema below, compactly, with no indentation and no line breaks, without a code fence or surrounding text. PHP assembles this response with the pinned fixed fields into the final MotoDesign record in format `moto-design-v3`.

Return every required AI-owned field with the correct type — a fixed part has `configurations: []`, a list with no items is `[]`. Do not add fields, invent source identifiers, omit required source objects or duplicate identifiers. The answer is used only when it satisfies the generation schema exactly: a value of the wrong type, a PHP-owned field or an item out of its place makes the whole answer unusable. Use `null`, basis `UNKNOWN` or `[]` only where the schema allows it. All references must resolve in the assembled design; forward references are allowed.

Provide the required DNA coverage mapping in `dna_coverage`, using the source keys supplied in `assembly.coverage` of the input: one entry for every key, naming the identity rules of this answer that carry that DNA item. This mapping is assembly metadata and is excluded from the final design.

`status` is `ok` when there is no blocking source issue, `partial` when a blocking source issue remains and the design record is complete, and `fail` only when the input itself is broken; then `data` is `{}`, `dna_coverage` is `[]` and `flags` names the fault. A long or difficult task does not establish `fail`.
