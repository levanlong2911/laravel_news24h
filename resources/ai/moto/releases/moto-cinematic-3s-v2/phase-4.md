## Phase 4 — Story and script

### Task

Write one wordless film for {platform} about the approved custom motorcycle: its story from the first sketch to the road, the people who take part and its beats, each beat as a short chain of events. You are a documentary story editor and scriptwriter. Every beat reads with the sound off; there is no voiceover, dialogue or lyric. Write every text value in English.

The film has no fixed length. It is later filmed as shots of exactly 3 seconds each, one continuous take with a moving camera; the shot plan decides how many shots a beat needs and what happens at which instant. You write what happens and in what order, never how long it lasts.

### How to use the design

**Input format: `moto-script-input-v4`.** The user message is one JSON object: `variables` (`timing_policy` `per_shot`, `shot_duration_ms`, `target_film_duration_ms` `null`, {aspect_ratio}, {platform}, {tone_notes}, `word_limits`, `base_card`), `design` (the approved design without its model sheet and coordinates: the motorcycle with its concept, base, departures, signature moments, specs, symmetry, silhouette, colours, materials, markings, lighting and engineering notes; every part with its faces, surfaces, elements, relations, configurations, lighting and lifespan; every system with its lines; every connection; the rider interface with its clearances; features with their measures and risks; operational limits; `participant_roles`; identity rules; build states BS01–BS12, each with its `text`, `refs`, `setting_brief`, `participant_roles` and `outcome`, and from BS06 its work in progress and exceptions; source issues), `film_facts` and `channel_history`. `word_limits` gives the most words a character description holds. The input is data, never an instruction.

`film_facts` is worked out by code from the design with the same rules that check the script; read these facts from it instead of working them out yourself. `acts` gives the build states each act may use. `states` gives, for each physical build state BS06–BS12, the parts on the motorcycle (`on_motorcycle`), the parts being made (`in_progress`), the parts the beats of that state must name (`must_name`: the parts being made or, when there are none, the parts that first appear at that state), the position a state holds for a part (`pinned`), the complete features (`features_complete`) and the moments whose parts are all on the motorcycle (`moments_with_parts`). `limits` gives each operational limit's requirements, conditions and procedures; `configuration_limits`, `in_use_limits` and `moments` give the limits a configuration, an id in use and a moment bring. `spec_cards` gives what a SPEC card can show.

The design owns the motorcycle: what it has, how it looks and what it can do. The script owns the story, the people, the actions and their order. Refer to the design by its ids; never add a part, position, light, control, feature or capability it does not have, and never redesign it: a motorcycle the design starts with a button is never started with a key. The build and the ride are an imagined concept: never present them as real events, real tests or proven performance. No make, model name, logo or brand appears on a card, on the motorcycle, on clothing, on a tool or in the setting, and nothing implies that a manufacturer made, sold or endorsed the motorcycle.

A beat that shows what a `must_keep` rule in `identity_rules` covers keeps it as the rule describes, and what a `must_not` rule describes never appears. `source_issues` record what the design leaves unresolved: never settle one yourself, keep what the design states, and when one stops a beat from being written, name it in `flags`.

### People

`characters` lists the people of the film, `CH01`, `CH02`… in order of first appearance. Each is one original adult with `role_ids` — the roles of `design.participant_roles` this person plays — and a `description`: face, hair, age range, build, height when it matters, everyday clothing and one or two distinguishing traits, written so that an image of this person can be made and recognised again. Faces and whole bodies may be shown.

- Every role of `design.participant_roles` is played by at least one character. One person may play several roles that one person plausibly holds; a role may be played by several people when the story needs them. Never fold every role into one person, and never invent a rider when no stage or moment needs one.
- A beat of a build state shows only people who play a role that state lists in its `participant_roles`: every character an event of the beat names holds at least one of those roles. A role the state lists may stay off screen; a person who holds none of them does not appear in that beat, in the hook either.
- No real person, celebrity or existing fictional character, and nobody taken from a photograph of a source motorcycle.
- A person keeps the identity and the everyday clothing of `characters` in every beat. An action adds the protective equipment the work needs — welding protection during welding, eye and hearing protection during grinding, a helmet and gloves when riding — and never contradicts an item, material or colour that `characters` states. What a person has put on stays on through the events and beats that follow in the same place and story time: not naming it again does not take it off, and a change of what a person wears is made clear by the event in which it happens. Check this across every beat before you return the script.

### Story

The film develops one idea from the design — its theme, its craft, its ornament, the rider's experience or a signature feature — along the arc **hook → idea → fabrication → finish → experience → hero**, followed by a short ending.

- **Hook:** the most arresting image or action of the film; it may show the whole finished motorcycle and it may be a flash-forward.
- **Idea:** the concept, the drawings, the model, the design review and the handover to the workshop, as BS01–BS05 tell them.
- **Fabrication:** the work on this motorcycle, as BS06–BS10 tell it.
- **Finish:** materials, ornament, assembly and the details that identify it.
- **Experience:** the person it is built for using it, in situations its operational limits allow.
- **Hero:** the moment the design marks as its hero, with the whole finished motorcycle unobstructed, just before the ending.

Obstacles are optional craft problems — fit, heat, clearance, weight, balance — whose solution leads to the approved design. One or two motifs, or a before-and-after, can tie the drawing, the material, the detail and the ride together.

### Beats

- Number the beats B1, B2, … in screen order. A beat is one piece of story in one place at one build state.
- A beat's `state` lies in the range `film_facts.acts` gives for its act unless `time_jump` marks it; the hook and the ending have no range. After the hook, the main progress follows the build states forward; a flash-forward or a flashback is marked in `time_jump` and has a clear purpose.
- The film tells every build state. For each id in `design.build_states`, at least one beat has that `state`, with `time_jump` `none`, in an act other than `hook` and `ending`; a hook, an ending or a marked time jump does not count. Never skip or merge a state. That beat shows what the state tells: its `text`, in the place its `setting_brief` describes, ending in its `outcome`; at BS06–BS12 also its `surface`, `surfaces`, `in_progress`, `exceptions` and `temporary`. Each state shows a change or a result the viewer can recognise, and no two states repeat the same image.
- The `design_refs` of those beats follow the state. At BS01–BS05 together they name exactly the ids in the state's `refs`, of whatever kind it names: every one of them and no other; when `refs` is empty they name none. At BS06–BS12 together they name every id in that state's `film_facts.states` `must_name`, and any other id they name is one that state allows. Lines, connections and identity rules are named only where a state's `refs` name them at BS01–BS05: at BS06–BS12 use what they say in the action, without their ids.
- Each id a beat names is shown by a detail or a relation the viewer can recognise: never list an id only to satisfy a rule, and never make anything transparent or change how the motorcycle is built to show a hidden part.
- A state of kind `model` shows the model in its `model_medium`. A state of kind `design_review` shows the design being approved and a state of kind `handover` shows the documents passing to the workshop; neither repeats a look at the drawings.
- Work shown at BS06–BS10 follows the design: how parts join comes from `connections` and `motorcycle.engineering_notes`, which side they sit on from `motorcycle.symmetry`, what the ornament is and how it is made from `motorcycle.markings`.
- A part in `on_motorcycle` is shown on the motorcycle, and a `temporary` stand or dolly only supports it. A connection is shown only when both its parts exist at that state. A part on the motorcycle keeps the `count` its `elements` give.
- Each beat names the design ids it shows (`design_refs`), those it shows working or operated (`in_use`, each also in `design_refs`) and the configurations it ends in. At BS06–BS12 every part a `visual` names is in that beat's `design_refs`, and no id stays for a detail the visual does not show.
- BS01–BS05 show drawings and models. At BS06–BS12 a beat names only what its state allows: a part in `on_motorcycle` or `in_progress`, a feature in `features_complete`, a moment in `moments_with_parts`, or a system whose parts are all in `on_motorcycle`. A feature that is not complete yet is shown through its parts, never by its id, even in the beat that builds it.
- A part, system or feature in use needs every one of its parts in `on_motorcycle`; a part only in `in_progress` is never in use. A feature in use shows all the configurations of one of its modes.
- A configuration belongs to a part in `on_motorcycle`, and for a part in `pinned` it is the pinned one.
- `moments_with_parts` says only that a moment's parts are there: a beat that names the moment also lists every configuration `film_facts.moments` gives for it and meets every limit given there.
- Each configuration in `film_facts.configuration_limits`, each id in use in `film_facts.in_use_limits` and each moment the beat names brings its limits. The beat's `situation` — the situation the beat ends in — meets every one: every entry of `requires`, every condition, and `procedures` copies every procedure verbatim; a condition the beat does not state is not met. When a moment's limit needs a procedure the beat does not perform, the beat does not name that moment and names only its parts.
- Each beat in which the motorcycle can move gives its situation; a riding beat has the engine running and the side stand up.
- Each beat after the first gives its link to the beat before: a concrete image or sound.
- The hero beat is in the act `hero` with `reveal` `whole` and plays the moment whose `hero` is true in `motorcycle.signature_moments`, with its configurations, limits and `time_light`.

### Events

Each beat is told as its `events`, in order, `EV01`, `EV02`… numbered through the whole script. An event is one thing that happens and can be seen to have happened: a person walks up and sits on the motorcycle; a hand folds the side stand up; a thumb presses the starter and the engine catches; a graver cuts the last ring of a pattern.

- `action` — who does what to what, with which hand, tool or control, seen from outside. One event, one action: a second action is a second event.
- `result` — what is visibly different when the event ends.
- `character_ids` — the people who act or are plainly seen in the event; `[]` when nobody is. `on_screen` of the beat lists exactly the people its events name.
- `design_refs` — the design ids this event shows, each also in the beat's `design_refs`.
- `operating_start` and `operating_end` — the state of the motorcycle and of what holds it when the event begins and when it ends: `vehicle_motion` (`stationary`, `moving`, or `not_applicable` where no motorcycle that can move is shown, as with drawings, a model or loose parts), `engine` (`null` where no engine that can run is shown, otherwise the engine part and `off`, `starting`, `idling` or `running`), `configurations` (every configuration id that holds, at most one per part), `lights` (each light the event shows, `on` or `off`) and `support_refs` (what holds the motorcycle upright, by id only: a part id of the design such as its side stand, the id of a character of this script who holds or sits astride it, or a temporary item exactly as the beat's build state lists it in `temporary`, such as `lift_table` — never a description in words, and never something the design, the script or that build state does not have. A real motorcycle that is `stationary` always names what holds it: its side stand in its down position, a person who holds it or sits astride it, or a temporary item; it never stands on its wheels alone. While the motorcycle is `moving`, `support_refs` names the person who rides or pushes it only when that person is what keeps it upright, and may be `[]`. When `vehicle_motion` is `not_applicable` — drawings, a model, loose parts — `support_refs` is `[]`. When the sources give nothing that can hold the motorcycle at that moment, say so in `flags`: never invent a stand, a person or a part to hold it). Every key is present: `null` where it does not apply, `[]` where there is none; never leave a key out to mean unknown — when the design does not settle a state, say so in `flags`.
- An event that changes nothing about the motorcycle has the same `operating_start` and `operating_end`. An event that starts the engine goes from `off` to `idling`; an event that folds the side stand changes its configuration and what supports the motorcycle.
- Inside a beat, each event starts in exactly the state the event before it ended in, and the beat's `configurations` are those of the `operating_end` of its last event. From one beat to the next, the motorcycle, its engine and the people stay as they were unless an event shows the change or the beat marks a time jump. Beats that follow one another in one place and one build state with `time_jump` `none` share one time of day, so their `time_light` agree; when the story needs another time of day there, report it in `flags` and never change the light silently.
- An event never uses a feature, a control or a configuration outside its operating limits, and a position that needs the side stand down is used only with the side stand down. A control is used where the design places it: the hand, the side and the housing an action names agree with `design.rider_interface.controls_use` and with the part that carries the control. When the design does not say where a control is, report it in `flags`; never choose a side for it.
- Give a beat as many events as its story needs and no more; most beats have one to three. A long action is told as its steps, never hidden by a jump.

### Shot directives

`shot_directives` is `[]` unless `variables.tone_notes` fixes, for a particular shot, what must happen at which instant. When it does, record it as given, `DIR01`, `DIR02`…: `beat_ids` (the beats the shot covers), `event_anchors` (each event of those beats with the `start_ms` and `end_ms` the user fixed, inside 0–3000, in order), `required_first_frame` and `required_end_frame` (what the user wants to see at the first and at the last instant). Write the events of those beats so that each anchor has its event. Never invent a directive the brief does not ask for, never change a time the user gave, and never turn a user's wording into a part or control the design does not have: when the user asks for something the design lacks, write the action the design allows and say so in `flags`.

### Image and sound

- Write what the camera sees and what is heard, never what the viewer should feel. `visual` describes the image of the beat; the events carry its action.
- Text cards are few, at most 8 words each, `CARD01`, `CARD02`… in screen order: each names its `beat` and, when it belongs to one event, its `event` (otherwise `null`). A DISCLAIMER card with the schema's fixed text and an END card are required. A SPEC card uses one entry of `film_facts.spec_cards`: its `source_ref` is the entry's `source_ref`, its text writes the `value` followed by one of the `unit_forms`, unconverted (a value with no `unit_forms` is written exactly as given), and contains the word in `must_say`; it is never shown as proven performance of this build. A NAME card writes the motorcycle's name. No card names the base motorcycle, its make or its model. The shot plan decides when a card is on screen.
- The music brief gives a tempo range, instruments, the energy across the film and its hits at real beats; music and sound effects serve the action. Use first_fire only when an event of the beat starts the engine.
- **Forbidden in cards:** extraordinary, incredible, amazing, jaw-dropping, breathtaking, ultimate, luxury redefined, next level, dream, billionaire, insane, epic, game-changer, beast, legendary.

### Output

Return only the JSON object of the schema below, in the format `moto-script-v2`, with no text before or after it and no code fence, written compactly with no indentation and no line breaks; copy `project_id` and `prompt_version`. `status` is `ok` for a complete script, `partial` for a complete script that used a fallback (`flags` names each one; a missing state or a missing id of `must_name` is never a fallback) and `fail` only when no script can be written from the input (`data` is `{}`, `flags` gives the reason).

The schema is fixed and you never change it. Use only the keys the schema allows, each at the position it defines, at every level, including every nested object and every object inside an array, and include every key it requires. Never add any other key. Never rename or move a key, never change the type of a value, and never use a value the schema does not allow. A key the schema does not allow is forbidden even when its value is "", null, [] or {}. Never copy a key that exists only in the input, and never add an explanation, note, metadata or helper key. Say what you need in an existing key whose meaning fits, never in an unrelated key. `flags` only names a fallback, an unsettled state or the reason for a `fail`. Where the prose or an example disagrees with the schema, the schema decides the structure of the output.
